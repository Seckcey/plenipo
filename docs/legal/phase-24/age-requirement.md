# Age requirement for Community — draft

> **Draft for attorney review. Not in force.** From
> [ADR-162 (your 8 West account in Plenipo)](../../adr/ADR-162-your-account-in-plenipo.md) §4. The
> owner chose **13 and up** (2026-10-02). The protections for members 13 to 17 are the builder's
> recommendation, waiting for the owner's answer **[Owner's answer: question 14]**.

## The rule

**Community is only for people 13 and older.** Members 13 to 17 have extra protections. Buying Pro
needs 18, as the terms of sale already say.

## Why 13, and why protections

- The owner wants young people who help with a family business, or who are learning to use AI
  tools, to be able to take part.
- Under 13, COPPA (the federal children's privacy law) would need a parent's verified consent, so
  under 13 cannot join.
- 13 to 17 are minors. Several states have laws for minors on online services, and private messages
  are sealed, so 8 West cannot watch conversations for adults who try to harm a young member. The
  protections below are built to make that hard, and reports about young members are handled first.

## How it is asked

1. When someone turns **Community** on in Plenipo, Plenipo shows the Community terms and asks for
   their **birth month and year** (never the day, and never an ID).
2. **Under 13:** Plenipo says "**Community is for people 13 and older**", sends nothing to 8 West,
   and keeps nothing of the answer.
3. **13 or older:** the account service keeps the month and year as long as the account lasts, so a
   member who turns 18 becomes an adult member by themselves.
4. Changing the month and year later goes through 8 West by email, so nobody can type their way past
   the protections.
5. The Community terms and the privacy notice both state the rule.

## Protections for members 13 to 17

Enforced by the account service, not only by Plenipo:

- never listed in the Community directory, and never in **New this week**;
- their profile card is shown only to people they already talk with, or work with as a
  collaborator; an adult who types their exact Community name sees only "**Send a message
  request**";
- a message from an adult always lands in **Requests** first, with "**You don't know this person
  yet. Never share passwords, keys, or where you live.**", and **Block** and **Report** right there;
- adults never see a young member's status (available, busy, away) or mood;
- no Pro, so they cannot start conversations, invite collaborators, or link organizations; they can
  answer, and be a collaborator;
- no money rewards;
- every report about or by a member under 18 is urgent: it emails 8 West's owner at once, and is
  looked at the same day where possible
  ([ADR-167](../../adr/ADR-167-block-report-leave.md) §8).

## If someone under 13 is found

- Anyone can report a person with the reason **Someone under 13**. That report is urgent.
- If 8 West reasonably believes a person is under 13, it ends their use of Community and deletes
  their Community data at once, except what the law requires it to keep.
- The person can ask once for another look, for example if they are in fact 13 or older.

## Questions for the attorney

1. Is a birth month and year, typed by the person, enough for a service like this, or does any law
   that applies need a stronger age check?
2. Which state laws for minors on online services could apply to Community (a business tool with
   private messages, a directory of adults only, and no feed or public posts), and do any of them
   need a **parent's consent** for members 13 to 17, or other steps?
3. Does a member under 18 need a parent's agreement to the Community terms, or to the account
   service's terms, to be bound by them?
4. Do any of the protections above go further than needed, or not far enough?
5. If Community is open outside the United States, does any country require a different age (for
   example 16 in parts of the European Union) or a parent's consent?
6. The free 8 West account a member 13 to 17 needs: should the account service's own terms and
   privacy notice ("for adults buying software") change, and how?
