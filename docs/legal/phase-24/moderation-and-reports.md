## How we keep Community safe

Plenipo Community is run by 8 West Ventures, LLC. This page says what you can do when someone
bothers you, how we handle reports, and what we may do about them.

### What you can do yourself, at any time

- **Block** a person. They can no longer message you, send you a link request, an invitation, or an
  objective, or find your profile. Any link or collaboration with them ends. They are not told.
- **Leave** a conversation, a link, or an organization you help with, or turn Community off.
- **Report** a person, a message, a profile, a link request, an invitation, or an objective.

### What a report sends us

Private messages are sealed: only you and the other person can read them, and we cannot. So when
you report messages or objectives, you choose which ones (up to 20), and your Plenipo sends us their
words with a proof that they are real. We see only what you send. For a profile, we look at the
profile itself. We never receive your files, your activity history, or your other conversations.

You choose a reason: spam; harassment or threats; a scam; hate; sexual content; someone under 13;
a risk to a young person; pretending to be someone else; or something else. You can add a note.

### Who looks at reports, and how fast

People at 8 West read every report, on 8 West's own secure admin site. Reports are not sent to AI
workers or AI companies. We look at each report within **2 business days**. Reports of **harassment
or threats**, **someone under 13**, or **a risk to a young person**, and every report about or by a
member under 18, are looked at the same day where possible.

### What we may do

When something breaks the Community terms, we may, with a reason each time:

- send a **warning**;
- **hide** a profile's picture, message, or name;
- **pause** someone's Community for 7 or 30 days;
- **end** someone's Community. Their profile and waiting messages are deleted, their links and
  collaborations end, and the same email cannot rejoin.

We choose the step that fits what happened, and how often it has happened before.

### What you hear back

- **If you reported:** we tell you when we are done, and whether we took action. We do not say what
  action, to protect everyone's privacy.
- **If you were reported and we took action:** we email you which rule and what we did.

### Asking for another look

If we took action against you, you can reply to our email within 14 days, once, to ask for another
look. If more than one person works on reports at 8 West, someone else looks. We tell you the result.

### The law, and when we contact authorities

- If a report shows apparent child sexual abuse material, we report it to the National Center for
  Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC), as United States law requires, and keep it only as the law
  requires. **[Attorney: 18 U.S.C. § 2258A duties, the time to keep it, and the wording.]**
- If a report shows a real threat to someone's life or safety, we may tell the police.
- **[Attorney: how 8 West handles requests from the police and courts, and whether and when it tells
  the person concerned.]** We can only give what we have. We cannot read sealed messages, and we keep
  little else (see the privacy notice for how long).

### How long we keep reports

A report, what it carried, and what we did are kept for 1 year after we close it, then deleted.

## Inside 8 West (not published): how a report is worked

1. The **Reports** page on the admin site lists open reports, oldest first, with the urgent reasons
   at the top. Urgent reasons also email the owner at once.
2. Each report shows: the reason and note; what was reported, and whether its proof checks out (a
   report whose proof fails is closed as "could not be confirmed"); both people's Community names;
   and earlier reports about the person reported.
3. The person working it picks an action from the list above and writes the reason. The service
   sends the emails, and the admin site records who did what, when (`admin_audit`).
4. Apparent child sexual abuse material: do not download or forward it; follow the attorney's NCMEC
   steps; the service keeps the report locked for the time the law requires.
5. An appeal goes to someone other than the first person, when there is one.
6. Once a month, the owner looks at the number of reports and how long they took. If 2 business days
   is not being met, the owner decides whether to add help.
