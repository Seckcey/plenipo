# v1.8.0 — Home, a page for everything, your own terminal, and pop-up notices (Phase 12)

Plenipo now opens on **Home**, where Pip greets you and says in a line how your company is doing.
From there, every department, project, worker, and task has its own page. A **terminal** panel lets
you type on this PC or on your servers, and shows each worker's server commands as they run.
Windows **pop-up notices** tell you when something needs you. **Settings** is in one tidy place.
And Plenipo wears its new brand: the Plenipo logo, and **Pip**.

Nothing you rely on changed how it works. Your organization, projects, workers, approvals,
permissions, servers, and switches are where you left them. Settings → Servers works as before.

## Highlights

- **Home.** Pip's greeting and how things are, then:
  - **Waiting for you:** requests for your OK and lessons to keep, with **Review**.
  - **What's stuck:** objectives that failed, refusals, handoffs that could not start, servers whose ID
    changed, and positions that can't work — each opens where you can fix it.
  - **Departments:** a card each, with its health, its Manager, its projects and workers, and its
    last 24 hours.
  - **Current objectives**, **Who's working**, and **Just finished** (with the first line of each
    answer).
  - Pip's pose follows what matters most: pointing the way when something waits for you, here to
    help when something is stuck, at the laptop while work goes on, celebrating finished work,
    resting when all is quiet.
- **A page for each department, project, worker, and task.** They open from Home, the
  Organization map, the Projects, Workers, and Activity pages, and each other, with **Back**. The
  top bar says where you are ("Department · Operations"), and **Showing** opens a department's or a
  project's page.
  - **Department:** its Manager, projects, who is working, its queue, and its history (the last day,
    the last week, and every event, a page at a time).
  - **Project:** its Supervisor, folder and repository, objectives, the task tree of each one,
    branches and pull requests, screenshots, and recent decisions.
  - **Worker** (a position): its role, its AI tool and model and why, what it is working on, the
    permissions it is using now, its conversation, and its history.
  - **Task:** the objective and what counts as done, the delegation tree, its activity as it
    happens, approvals, screenshots and pull requests, decisions, and the result.
  - Plenipo comes back to the page you were on after a restart.
- **Your terminal** (the **Terminal** button in the top bar, or **Ctrl+`**). A panel at the bottom
  or on the right that you can resize; it remembers how you left it.
  - **This PC:** Windows PowerShell in your home folder, as you, never as administrator. Settings →
    Terminal can pick PowerShell 7 or Command Prompt.
  - **Your servers:** the servers in Settings → Servers, while **Remote computers (SSH)** is on. The
    server's pinned ID is checked first, and a changed ID is refused before Plenipo signs in.
    Plenipo signs in without ever showing the key or password. Production servers are red.
  - **Watch tabs:** when a worker connects to a server, a tab opens showing each command Guard let
    through and its output as it arrives, with **Stop** (the command running now) and
    **Disconnect**. It stays readable after the worker disconnects.
  - What you type is never recorded; the Activity trail says only that a terminal opened and
    closed. Workers never type into your terminal (while a worker uses your screen, mouse, and
    keyboard, the terminal takes no typing until you take over), and **Stop all** never closes
    it.
  - Turning **Remote computers (SSH)** off closes your terminals on servers. Closing the window
    while a terminal is open keeps Plenipo running in the tray, like running work; quitting
    closes your terminals.
- **Pop-up notices.** Windows tells you when a worker waits for your OK, a website check waits for
  you, something goes wrong (an objective failed, a server's ID changed, an AI tool signed out or
  reached its usage limit), work is finished, or a worker learned something. Several at once become
  one notice, and the same one is not repeated within a minute.
- **Settings in one place.** A list of sections on the left, one at a time, and the last one comes
  back: AI tools (and their sign-in), AI models, Permissions, Organization, Servers, Switches,
  **Notifications** (choose each kind, "only while Plenipo's window is not in front", and **Send a
  test notice**), **Terminal**, Personalization, **Local paths** (where Plenipo keeps its files),
  Diagnostics (a summary, and the technical page), and **About Plenipo**.
- **Every page from one set of building blocks.** The older pages' buttons, status marks, tabs,
  and cards now come from the design system, so every page looks and works the same.
- **Plenipo's brand.** The approved Plenipo logo (with Pip on the n) at the top of the strip, the
  three-rail P as the app icon, and Pip on Home, beside "nothing here yet", in the terminal panel,
  and in Settings → About.

## Decisions

- [ADR-031: the terminal panel](https://github.com/Seckcey/plenipo/blob/main/docs/adr/ADR-031-terminal-panel.md)
  — **accepted**, and built in this release.
- [ADR-033: Home, a page for each thing, pop-up notices, and Settings in one place](https://github.com/Seckcey/plenipo/blob/main/docs/adr/ADR-033-pages-notices-settings.md)
  — **proposed.** Accepting it means you agree with how this release put Phase 12 together: the
  worker page is about a position, Showing opens a page, notices are decided by Plenipo (never by
  the page), local paths are shown, not changed, and the terminal's few extra rules (a reload
  closes your terminals, and while a worker controls the screen the terminal takes no typing).
- [ADR-025: servers over SSH, through Guard](https://github.com/Seckcey/plenipo/blob/main/docs/adr/ADR-025-servers-over-ssh.md)
  — still **proposed**.

## Upgrading from 1.7.0

No Ledger migration. Plenipo opens on Home the first time, then on the page you were on. Notices
start on, and only while Plenipo's window is not in front; the terminal panel starts hidden.

## Known limits

- **Not yet checked on Windows by you.** Checked end to end on Linux with the real app, stand-in AI
  tools, and a synthetic SSH server; Windows builds and runs the tests in CI. Please check on
  Windows: the terminal on this PC (PowerShell) and on a real server, and a real notice (Settings →
  Notifications → **Send a test notice**). The Phase 11 Windows check against a real server is also
  still to do
  ([Phase 11 checklist](https://github.com/Seckcey/plenipo/blob/main/docs/phases/phase-11-checklist.md)).
- **Notices need Windows to allow them.** If none appears, check Windows Settings → System →
  Notifications for Plenipo.
- The terminal on a server is for Linux and Unix servers (Windows servers come in Phase 15).

## Install

Download `Plenipo_1.8.0_x64-setup.exe` below and run it. It is a per-user install with no admin
rights needed, signed as **8 West Ventures, LLC**. If SmartScreen still warns, choose **More info →
Run anyway**.
